Risk-parity blend of the three sleeves (each scaled to the same volatility), net of measured spreads and the overnight financing of every day held. The sleeves are genuinely orthogonal (monthly correlations of −0.10 to +0.17 between them), which is why the combined book beats every sleeve on its own. Run at 10% annual volatility, its worst peak-to-trough was −26%. Before 2023 the blend's Sharpe was 0.97; the held-out figure is flattered by an unusually good recent stretch for index and gold trends.

Revised 3 October 2026. A full re-test after we found and fixed several bugs: three strategy engines compared different instruments when our price files were sorted by date; the live bots acted on stop and target exits one candle late; financing was mis-modelled (one tool over-charged FX six-fold, others applied today's rates to all of history), and is now charged at historical rates for every day held; and the commodities bot could not trade markets that were shut at its run time for about a month. Every number on this page now uses the corrected engines, measured spreads and full financing, on OANDA's costs (on IG's lower costs the blend is 1.41). The construction is simpler than August's (equal risk per trade in each book), so the figures are not directly comparable. The live record below is what actually matters.

How the backtests were validated (and what they can't tell you)

  • 15 years of data (2011–2026), every strategy tested net of estimated spread costs.
  • Every edge must be profitable in both halves of the sample independently. An edge that only worked in one era is discarded, not averaged in.
  • Cost stress test: the FX book stays profitable with spreads doubled.
  • We rebuilt the indices book from scratch after finding inflated features in an early version. The numbers above are from the clean rebuild, which cost us ~0.1 Sharpe and is exactly why you can trust the rest.
  • What backtests can't prove: the future. That's what the live demo record below is for.
